`observer.cpp::now_ns()` uses `std::chrono::steady_clock`, converted to
nanoseconds. These timestamps are monotonic host elapsed-time readings; they are

Process duration and supervisor duration are different enclosing observations.
The analyzer does not subtract supervisor delay or observer overhead.

## Allocation-policy comparison

The reference policy is `per_operation`; the candidate is
`reusable_single_tile`. Both execute the same frozen 8×4×8 matmul, with the same
input fixture, expected FP32 output bits, poison alternation and explicit serial
close discipline. They differ in the already implemented allocation policy:

| Policy | Required observed allocation behavior | Primary measurement consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `per_operation` | Three payload buffers per operation; 99 creates and 99 releases across all 33 operations. No live payload buffers after each explicit close. | Per-operation allocation/release work is included in each timed submit/close interval. |
| `reusable_single_tile` | Three buffers retained across the owner lifetime, with stable addresses and 320 requested tensor-payload bytes; three final releases at owner teardown. | Resident allocation cost is paid at context creation, outside the primary intervals; reuse is what the warm experiment asks about. |

This intentionally tests warm repeated use. A favorable result would not prove
improvement for one-shot cold context creation, context teardown, larger tensor
shapes, concurrent contexts, a different allocator or another hardware backend.
The 320-byte figure is requested tensor payload, not a claim about complete
process, allocator or physical-device memory consumption.

Each measured policy batch is a separate fresh process. The producer's frozen
plan alternates policy order; it does not execute both policies in the same
context or carry one process's resident buffers into another. The prime and
warmup pair are retained diagnostics, not replacement or selectable samples.

## Counts, units and reduction

The source admits exactly 33 completed runs per process, checks 32 FP32 words
per run, and requires zero mismatches. The retained population therefore has:

- 24 × 33 = **792 launches**, and 792 × 32 = **25,344 checked words**.
- 24 excluded prime operations.
- Four excluded pair-zero warmup processes, each of which still contains its
  prime and 32 further operations.
- Twenty measured processes × 32 intervals = **640 measured operations**,
  with **20,480 checked words** in that measured population.

`p06_qualified_warm_analyze.py::analyze_population()` requires all 33 positive
durations and exactly the 32-element suffix, then computes the exact rational
batch mean `sum(durations[1:]) / 32`. Five measured batch means per policy per
format enter the frozen arithmetic. The median comparison uses integer
cross-products; every measured pair retains the host and cycle nonregression
requirements. There is no pooling of all operation durations as independent
statistical samples. The arithmetic's 16,384 useful-FLOP denominator is
32 × (2 × 8 × 4 × 8); it is an accounting normalization, not measured physical
throughput.

The `cycles` descriptor in `ai_bench_common/counters.h` selects core zero's
`VX_CSR_MCYCLE`, with unit `active_core_cycles`. It is distinct from the
`SimPlatform::cycles()` value stored as lifetime `ticks` in
`ai_counter_preflight/hooks.cpp::model_locked()`. Lifetime records also retain
core cycles, reset generation, activity, pending reads and terminal state.
Neither clock is the host nanosecond timer. The selected cycle difference is a
modeled cumulative batch observation; it is not a direct per-kernel duration,
calibrated physical frequency, FPGA wall time, power or energy measurement.

The existing counter/lifetime validators require snapshot and run/reset/idle
relationships, available selector replies, pending-read drainage and complete
disposal. Those obligations are unchanged by pair/phase admission. The new
route must establish them independently for every one of the 24 processes;
the earlier four-process qualification does not transfer as execution evidence.
