## Boundary findings

- Proof bytes must match the frozen byte length and SHA-256 before decoding.
  Parsed objects also require the frozen semantic digest. Strict decoding
  rejects duplicate keys, floats, nonfinite numbers and oversized integer
  tokens; object validation bounds depth, arrays, strings and unsigned integer
  values. Source descriptors reject duplicate or noncanonical relative paths,
  unexpected fields, unsorted inventories and Boolean byte counts.
- Live proof and admission inputs require canonical absolute host paths,
  regular files, no symlink traversal, one hardlink and bounded sizes. Their
  opened-file identities are checked around reads. Admission additionally
  compares the final pathname identity and rechecks proof, source, helper,
  archive and dependency identities at later stability checkpoints.
- The reused, fixed-hash archive reader opens only the caller-selected original;
  verifies its whole-file digest before parsing and at close; bounds central
  directory, member count, expanded data and aggregate reads; and rejects
  duplicate members, traversal, absolute/backslash paths, symlink members and
  file/directory shadowing. It does not extract members or execute retained
  Python, binaries or commands. Recorded producer paths remain syntactic data.
  The inherited read-only archive utility allows host-file hardlinks, unlike
  live admission; its descriptor/path stability checks and final full hash
  continue to bind the selected bytes. No write-through or acceptance bypass
  was identified from that difference.
- The qualified reader requires explicit caller-selected proof and prerequisite
  identities. It compares all data members against the two selected archive
  roots, reconstructs summaries through the actual prerequisite entry points,
  and checks its trusted helper closure again before returning. The shared
  recursive `_same` comparison requires exact types, including nested Boolean
  fields. The historical reader cannot select this route from packet contents.
- CLI output is a caller-selected path, never a retained producer path. The
  reader completes verification before opening output, and exclusive `"x"`
  creation prevents overwriting an existing file. Rejected replay creates no
  output. Parent directories are caller-controlled; the CLI does not claim
  containment of that explicitly chosen destination.

The selection covers the complete 20-test contract suite; admission path and
proof-link controls; retained execution/producer-path guards; exact archive

route isolation. Five independent tests additionally exercise real malformed
ZIP containers, a symlinked host archive, successful CLI output, rejection of an
existing output, and absence of output after rejected replay. The malformed ZIP
test includes five distinct traversal/duplicate/symlink/absolute/backslash

The CLI tests mock replay only to isolate the output branch. Synthetic complete
reader controls explicitly mock dependency and historical prerequisite
verification; they reconstruct retained raw transcripts but are not fresh native
execution. This review did not rerun the full historical originals, run a GPU
process, authenticate a producer, or establish warm/performance/phase acceptance.
