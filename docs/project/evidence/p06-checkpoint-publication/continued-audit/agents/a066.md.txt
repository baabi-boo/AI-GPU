## What a successful source-matched workflow establishes

The workflow is a fresh dependency build followed by native lifetime and
separate controls, not merely a cache-integrity screen. It selects Ubuntu
22.04 and CPython 3.12.14, restores only the pinned toolchain, records job/workflow
identity, and checks the 15 GiB disk/4 GiB available-memory admission bounds.
Preparation and all execution stages receive the provider's exact `GITHUB_SHA`.
See `.github/workflows/p06-dependency-provenance.yml:105–233`.

The locked source set is SoftFloat, Ramulator, yaml-cpp, spdlog and argparse.
The three selected products are `softfloat.a`, `libramulator.so` and
`libramulator-vortex.so`. The plan has 406 translation units: 309 SoftFloat,
60 Ramulator, 29 yaml-cpp, seven spdlog and one extension. The receipt reader
requires exactly **406 compile, three archive, two index and two link records**
—413 tool operations—with unique identities and the complete locked graph.
It checks objects, archive members, links and exact staged product/source
population. These are execution requirements; this review did not run them.
See `p06_dependency_contract.py:12–78` and
`p06_dependency_receipt.py:787–944` under `ci/project/`.

The builder requires fresh source/build directories, clean exact-revision
measurement source, pinned preparation and host-tool identities. It retains
the loaded local helper inventory, sources, command/tool records, build inputs
and products. It repeats helper/tool/source/staging stability checks around
commands and at finalization, checks the source checkout again after staging,
then validates its completed original receipt through the inert reader.
See `p06_dependency_build.py:109–125,272–313,427–534`.

The workflow pins the **actual original receipt bytes** by SHA-256 and size
before native execution. Admission checks the exact source revision, selected
measurement root, staged product identities, helper files and tool/source
stability; model-link validation joins those products to the actual application
build. The native runner binds the receipt to each variant and process,
verifies expected loaded libraries in each native output, and repeats closing
stability checks. The complete managed population is **four processes,
132 launches and 4,224 checked FP32 words** across both formats and allocation
policies. `dependency_source_to_loaded_binary_verified` requires the complete
passing lifetime population and both admitted variant builds. See workflow
`:234–276`, `p06_dependency_admission.py:79–254` and
`p06_managed_preflight_execute.py:465–471,604–630,680–747,779–833`.

Separate later workflow steps require the positive pending-read observation,

## Claims that remain false

The contract fixes seven flags to false: `qualified`, `ready_for_warm`,
`warm_execution`, `full_warm_coverage`, `acceptance_claimed`,
`phase_acceptance` and `performance_acceptance`. The builder initializes them
false, the workflow requires their exact false values when pinning the original,
and the receipt/admission readers reject a changed claim. Thus a green run can
establish its bounded fresh source-to-loaded-product prerequisite without
establishing complete 40-component counter qualification, an absolute no-wrap
bound, the qualified-warm experiment or performance/phase acceptance. Complete
compiler/system-input closure and malicious concurrent-writer protection are
also outside the stated contract. See `p06_dependency_contract.py:56–86`,
workflow `:249–257`, `p06_dependency_receipt.py:690–702`, and
`p06_dependency_admission.py:79–93`.

The separate inert readers report `native_execution="not_run"` and
`retained_code_executed=false`. Their consistency result is not a new native
execution or producer authentication. The upload step uses `if: always()` and
retains the original evidence after failure as well; a partial artifact does
not become a completed successful receipt. See workflow `:298–307`,
`p06_dependency_replay.py:175–184` and
`p06_dependency_native_replay.py:879–885`.
