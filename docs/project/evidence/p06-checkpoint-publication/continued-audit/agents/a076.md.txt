## Recipient and encryption scope

`p06_qualified_warm_encrypt.py:82–104` accepts exactly one canonical lowercase
X25519 `age1` public address. It checks the fixed alphabet/length, 32-byte
encoding padding and Bech32 checksum. This is syntax/encoding validation,
not proof that the intended reviewer controls the matching private identity.
Hosted startup validates that address and performs an actual trusted-age
recipient probe before native work (`p06_qualified_warm_hosted.py:592–603`).
The guide requires the owner to verify the matching private identity outside
Actions (`p06-hosted-warm-guide.md:114–119`). No private identity is an Actions
input, and SSH/plugin/passphrase recipients are outside this route.

The encryptor streams the selected private tree through the explicitly trusted
age executable; it writes no intermediate plaintext tar. The encrypted tar
includes a canonical manifest and the complete selected `private/` tree,
including hidden regular files and empty directories. Framing/length checks
catch accidental plaintext or an unexpected executable's output; they do not
cryptographically authenticate an otherwise untrusted age binary
(`p06_qualified_warm_encrypt.py:490–506, 625–666`). Input and output bounds and the
retention reserve remain separate from native admission's 15 GiB disk gate.

## Public output and release gate

The public summary has exactly ten fields: `schema_version`, `contract_id`,
`expected_revision`, `outcome`, `encryption_passed`, `file_count`,
`directory_count`, `input_bytes`, `ciphertext_bytes`, and `ciphertext_sha256`
(`p06_qualified_warm_encrypt.py:562–569`). These are retention identities and
counts. They do not report native process/launch counts, completeness, replay
qualification or performance acceptance, and expose no plaintext inventory or
private diagnostic content.

Before publication, the encryptor requires successful bounded age completion,
completed process cleanup, stable selected inputs/tool identity, and a reread
of the ciphertext. After the no-clobber public pair commits, it checks each
named output's owned inode, regular-file status, exact byte count and SHA-256,
including descriptor/named-path stability (`p06_qualified_warm_encrypt.py:517–559,
658–704`). A committed pair can survive a subsequent interruption; its mere
existence does not establish a successful encryption invocation or durable
hosted delivery.

The hosted gate independently requires exactly the two allowed public files,
reconstructs the expected summary from the selected retention inventory,
checks actual ciphertext size/hash, and requires exact equality with the
encryption command's terminal summary (`p06_qualified_warm_hosted.py:430–446`).
Only successful execution of that gate sets `encrypted_retention_ready`.
The workflow's always-run upload step requires that output and enumerates only
`qualified-warm-retention.tar.age` and `encryption-summary.json`
(`p06_qualified_warm_hosted.py:675–687, 716–723`;
`.github/workflows/p06-qualified-warm.yml:88–100`). A successful artifact upload
must still be observed before claiming durable delivery.

## Complete, partial and unresolved attempts

`writers_finished` checks exact Boolean launch disposition, completed cleanup,
finished acquisition/supervisor receipts and the started child-command
receipts. Unknown starts, missing/unreadable/unfinished receipts or unresolved
cleanup fail the observation and block packaging/encryption
(`p06_qualified_warm_hosted.py:449–480, 649–679`). This is checked operational
evidence, not an OS-level proof that every possible writer is absent.

Complete native success selects `success` packaging; a finished failed attempt
selects `partial`. Even failed packaging may leave selected private bytes that
can be encrypted after all writers finish. Hosted overall success still
requires native success **and** successful packaging **and** successful
encryption. Therefore valid encrypted partial retention may upload while the
attempt and job remain failed (`p06_qualified_warm_hosted.py:665–687`).

The complete original has exactly the `dependency-build/` and `qualified-warm/`
roots; the default complete reader rejects the partial layout. An explicitly
selected partial subtree is subject to every original semantic check and
cannot gain completeness merely through a prefix. Packaging failure may
preserve only already-copied bytes; encryption cannot establish that every
failure original was captured (`p06-hosted-warm-guide.md:239–301`).

The sibling `public.PRIVATE/`, raw originals, plaintext logs, final private
attempt receipt and other unselected runner-local inputs must stay private.
Their presence on an ephemeral runner is not durable retention. The selected
encrypted tree and the excluded private files are explicitly documented
(`p06-hosted-warm-guide.md:288–301, 357–381`).

## Required private restoration and independent native replay

The reviewer first checks downloaded ciphertext identity and the actual
execution revision, then decrypts privately with the matching held identity.
Nonzero decryption exits can leave partial plaintext; that output is rejected
as a usable archive. Successful age decryption alone is insufficient: the
reviewer records a separate plaintext tar digest and uses trusted reader code
to inspect the complete manifest/member inventory before restoration into a
fresh absent private directory (`p06-hosted-warm-guide.md:399–507`).

An optional encryption summary is only a consistency input to the plaintext
reader. That reader does not re-read ciphertext or verify its relationship to
plaintext. Its explicit output leaves `origin_authenticated`, native,
performance and phase acceptance false and records no native execution
(`p06_qualified_warm_retention.py:389–445`; restore false-claim constants at
`p06_qualified_warm_restore.py:71–83`).

A separate complete native original replay must then reconcile the original
57 native command receipts, 24 process transcripts and all frozen source,
dependency, prerequisite and numerical obligations. A valid complete replay can
still have an unfavorable performance hypothesis. Retention, restoration and
replay do not turn a failed or partial attempt into acceptance
(`p06-hosted-warm-guide.md:729–758`).

Existing recipient/publication/interruption/partial-retention and hosted
writer/public-output tests were inspected for these boundaries, not run by
this audit. No claim about actual native encryption, delivery or restoration
follows from this source review.
