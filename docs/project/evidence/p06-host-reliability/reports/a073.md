**Reclamation audit.** Normal trace destruction occurs only at macro completion
(`core.cpp:950–954`), retirement (`1215–1219`) and completed store-copy dispatch

`simobject_functional.h:200–216,1331–1361`). No other trace destructor/deallocator
or pool sweep was found.

**Retention versus leakage.** `sw/common/mempool.h:139–175` deliberately keeps
per-thread pools alive and lets later threads adopt them. Reusable pool chunks
and registry capacity alone are not a defect. Allocation removes a slot from
the free list; only explicit deallocation returns it (`48–74`). This trace
never returns. Its constructor owns six vector buffers in the declared profile:
source-register storage, outer source-data storage, three inner source vectors
and destination data (`instr_trace.h:92–96`; `constants.h:72`). No destructor
releases them. Repeated serial failed contexts consume reusable slots, then
unreleased heap fallbacks; registry adoption cannot recover either. A leak
detector may classify pooled storage as reachable, so RSS/exit reports alone
would not distinguish the issue.
